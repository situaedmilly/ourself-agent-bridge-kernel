# Hardware Identity Invariants v0.1

1. Every physical substrate has one canonical hardware-instance identifier.
2. Hardware identifiers are grounded in actual substrate evidence, not a hostname or user-supplied label alone.
3. Every OS instance has a distinct OS-instance identifier and an explicit host-substrate binding.
4. Every virtual substrate has a distinct guest identifier and an explicit host boundary.
5. Every access surface has its own identifier, authentication contract, and target binding.
6. Every ecosystem reality has its own identity and an explicit, evidence-backed binding to its substrate or service.
7. Aliases are aliases. They do not create additional physical hardware.
8. A binding is a first-class relation with status, evidence references, scope, and authority. A path diagram is not proof of a binding.
9. Device identity, OS identity, boot identity, account identity, and network identity remain separate.
10. Identity verification requires independent evidence and a declared verification method.
11. No credential, private key, secret, or authentication token is stored in the public registry.
12. Evidence expiry, revocation, replacement, and conflict must be represented explicitly.
13. Unresolved identity or jurisdiction blocks operational admission.
14. No compiler output is a signed receipt unless a real signing and independent verification process has been performed.
